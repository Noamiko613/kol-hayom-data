 halakha 1: כל איש ואישה שיעשה כל מלאכה מארבעים ושתים-minus-one, כלומר, שלשים ותשע מלאכות אבות, או אחת משאר המלאכות התלויות בהן, سواءً كانت גדולה או קטנה, violate the prohibition of Shabbat.  
 halakha 2: From where do we derive this? From the verse: "Six days shall you work, and on the seventh day you shall rest; even in plowing and in harvesting you shall rest" (Exodus 34:21). And the Sages said: "From 'in plowing and in harvesting' I only know these two [activities]; how do I know [that the prohibition applies to] all the activities performed in the construction of the Tabernacle? The verse states: 'Six days shall you work,' [indicating that] all laborious activities [are prohibited]."  
 halakha 3: And which are the activities performed in the construction of the Tabernacle? They are: sowing, plowing, reaping, binding sheaves, threshing, winnowing, selecting, grinding, sifting, kneading, baking, shearing wool, washing wool, beating wool, dyeing wool, spinning, weaving, making two loops, weaving two threads, separating two threads, tying, untying, sewing two stitches, tearing for the purpose of sewing two stitches, trapping a deer, slaughtering it, flaying it, salting it, curing its hide, scraping it, cutting it up, writing two letters, erasing for the purpose of writing two letters, building, demolishing, extinguishing a fire, kindling a fire, striking with a hammer, and anything similar to these activities.  
 halakha 4: These are the forty categories of activities that are prohibited on Shabbat, corresponding to the activities performed in the construction of the Tabernacle.  
 halakha 5: Anyone who performs any of these activities, whether intentionally or unintentionally, is liable for the consequences.  
 halakha 6: The Torah distinguishes between different levels of violation, depending on the intent and the specific activity performed.  
 halakha 7: It is forbidden to perform these activities not only in the usual manner but also in any unusual manner that achieves the same result.  
 halakha 8: The prohibition applies equally to men and women, and to converts who join the Jewish people.  
 halakha 9: Children who have not yet reached the age of religious responsibility are exempt from these prohibitions, although they should be educated in their observance.  
 halakha 10: The laws of Shabbat are fundamental to Jewish life and require careful study and observance.
